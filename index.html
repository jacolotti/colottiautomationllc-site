Automated 14-Point Fastener Inspection & Quality Interlock

Integrated a Keyence VS vision camera into an existing production fixture to verify the presence of 14 fasteners on each part, without adding time to the production cycle.

  • Whole-part inspection: Checks all 14 required fastener locations for presence.
  • No added cycle time: Inspection runs within the existing machine cycle.
  • Automatic containment: A failed inspection keeps the part locked in its fixture and generates an HMI fault.
  • Controlled removal: A qualified individual must badge in to authorize removal of a failed part.

Customer and facility details withheld. The system verifies fastener presence; it does not claim to measure fastening torque or joint integrity.

Completed project · Vision, robotics & error-proofing

Automated Finished-Goods Inspection & Robotic Quality Control

Converted an existing finished-goods bin stand into an automated inspection station. Integrated a Keyence IV4 vision sensor and two Keyence laser sensors with the main cell PLC, coordinating inspection and part-handling interlocks with the robot.

  • Welded-stud inspection: Checks each placed part for the presence of four drawn-arc welded studs.
  • Multi-layer bin handling: Uses program switching and multiple master images to support bins containing 85 parts, with a layer divider after every 25 parts.
  • Placement flexibility: Configured an approximately 6 × 6-inch inspection window to accommodate normal part-position variation.
  • One-piece flow: Implemented sequential part handling and inspection coordinated with the main cell PLC and robot.
  • Red Rabbit challenge testing: Developed a randomized test sequence that targets the next scheduled bin placement position.
  • HMI operator guidance: Added a visual indicator showing the next placement position; the same position outline identifies the location of a failed inspection.

Anonymized completed integration. Inspection verifies the specified visual stud features; no weld-strength measurement is claimed.

Common Problems We Help Solve

Most manufacturing automation issues are not caused by one single component. The challenge is usually identifying the right direction quickly and avoiding wasted time chasing the wrong problem.

Machines Randomly Faulting

Recurring production faults, intermittent machine behavior, unexplained downtime, and systems that are difficult to troubleshoot efficiently.

Vision Systems Causing Downtime

Inspection systems creating production instability, false rejects, difficult recoveries, inconsistent reads, or integration concerns.

Robot & Cell Recovery Issues

Production cells that are difficult to recover, robot process instability, sequencing concerns, or manufacturing coordination problems.

Conveyor & Flow Problems

Material handling bottlenecks, inconsistent flow, poor line coordination, conveyor timing issues, and throughput limitations.

Pneumatic Application Concerns

Air usage issues, cylinder sizing confusion, force concerns, inconsistent motion, and manufacturing performance problems.

Production Reliability Problems

Systems that technically function but continuously create production headaches, operator frustration, scrap, or downtime risk.
